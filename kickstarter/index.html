THE BREAKTHROUGH

We spent three years trying to design a card game.
Then we discovered a language.

Most card games begin with a problem: both people need the same game, the same knowledge, and usually their own deck. CORE7 begins with the opposite promise.

“I carry one box. I meet anyone. We can play.”

The cards use four colors, four symbols, and twenty-eight human words. A child can understand the battle. An adult can keep talking after the match. A team can use the same cards to plan, reflect, or name what is missing.

CORE7 · THE DEFAULT GAME

Four colors. One cycle.

  2. 02
    Choose in secret.

    Both players commit one card face down.

  3. 03
    Reveal together.

    The color cycle resolves instantly. Gray blocks.

  4. 04
    Manage what remains.

    The winner discards one card. The loser discards the played card plus one more.

  5. 05
    Win three rounds.

    Every revealed card becomes information. The match gets sharper as the hand gets smaller.

The rules are simple enough to teach without language.

FAQ

The questions we would ask before backing.

Do both players need a box?

No. That is the central promise. One box can build both hands, and the base game can be played with any four-color substitutes.

Why buy the cards if the game is free?

Because the physical deck is the art, the language, the portable ritual and the object you will actually want to put on a table. The mechanic is open. The artifact earns the purchase.

Do the Thai words affect gameplay?

No. Color resolves CORE7. The Thai text preserves the cultural origin and emotional meaning of each keyword. It can deepen conversation without creating a language barrier.

Is this an oracle or fortune-telling deck?

It can be used for symbolic reflection with an AI, but it does not claim to predict the future. The system is designed to create questions, perspectives and actions — not certainty.

Does rarity make a card stronger?

Never. Foil, art variants and rarity are collectible treatments only. A handwritten RED card can defeat the most expensive GREEN card ever printed.

Can children play?

The color game is intentionally simple and icon-supported. Adult supervision and age guidance for the final physical product will be confirmed after blind playtests.

Can I create my own cards or games?

That is part of the long-term system. The Open Creator Kit stretch goal formalizes schemas and compatibility guidance while preserving the four-color core.

Will there be more sets?

Yes: new keywords, art, artists and themes. The protocol remains Red, Blue, Green and Gray so the First Hand never becomes obsolete.
