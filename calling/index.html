Competitive edge · proposed

Win before the first card is played

Less guessing.
More evidence.

HALO cannot guarantee a win. The advantage is arriving with fewer unknowns: your paper matches, online practice, deck version, matchup plan and real performance history can meet in one place before Game 1 begins.

Without HALO

Going first or second is remembered from feeling. Sideboard or equipment notes live somewhere else. Online reps and paper reps are separate. Peak damage is a story you tell yourself.

With HALO

Load the matchup, see your real first/second split, pull the sideboard or equipment plan from the linked deck source, and combine supported online practice data with paper results.

Matchup plan on demand

Deck links can carry the exact version plus matchup notes: first/second preference, sideboard or equipment plan, and team or community guide recommendations.

Online practice import

Where an online platform provides an export, API or supported connection, HALO can bring those training sessions into the same competitive history instead of leaving them in another silo.

Success roadmap · proposed

A believable path to global scale

From one table in Bangkok
to a network of players.

HALO does not need to become a giant platform on day one. Each stage makes the next stage easier: a great physical product gets onto tables, persistent data creates retention, stores reduce the cost of trying it, and teams plus community guides create reasons to bring friends in.

1 · Win the table

Replace the phone with a better physical experience: metal control, fast setup and wireless sync.

2 · Own the history

Player ID makes every match useful even when the device is borrowed from a friend or store.

3 · Build intelligence

Deck versions, online imports, first/second data, matchup plans and community guides make the product more valuable over time.

4 · Win stores + teams

Lending pools create discovery. Team packs create multi-unit sales and shared competitive data.

5 · Become a global tabletop layer

Add more game presets, regions, community knowledge and competitive partnerships. The hardware can stay simple while the network around it becomes harder to replace.
