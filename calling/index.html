Not another

Why hardware?

The phone works.
This feels better.

The strongest pitch is not that HALO can do things a phone cannot. It is that a purpose-built object can make the match cleaner, faster and more intentional — then preserve what happened after the table is cleared.

Phone life tracker

  • Notifications and other apps live on the same screen
  • Often one shared phone or disconnected phones
  • The match usually disappears when the app closes
  • Touch-only interaction
  • Built for everything, not for the tabletop
VS

HALO

  • One purpose: stay with the match
  • Linked devices can share state wirelessly
  • Undo and history create a persistent match trail
  • Physical rotary input + touch
  • Can connect the physical match to a player profile later

Player identity layer · proposed

The mobile app has a bigger job

The device can be borrowed.
The history should still be yours.

The app should not exist only to make setup easier. It can be the player's identity, archive and statistics locker — separating who owns the hardware from who owns the match data.

01 · OWNED

My HALO

Your personal device can remember your profile and sync matches into your own history automatically.

02 · BORROWED

Store / friend HALO

Claim a session with your player profile before or after the match. Borrow the hardware without mixing its statistics into the lender's account.

03 · LINKED

Opponent identity

When two claimed player profiles link their HALOs, the match can know who played whom without manual re-entry later.

Competitive layer · proposal from an experienced tournament player

The opportunity beyond life tracking

A match is not just a result.
It belongs to a deck.

Competitive players do not only ask “Did I win?” They ask which deck, which version, which matchup, which day, and whether the latest change actually improved the list. HALO can preserve that structure without forcing paper players to log every card played.

External ecosystem · proposed

Do not rebuild what players already use

Paper data can live beside
online practice.

Every major TCG already has community tools for deck construction. HALO only needs to store the deck identity or URL. For Flesh and Blood, a Fabrary deck link is already usable as an input for Talishar online play, which makes the same deck link a natural bridge between paper testing and digital practice.

Paper match record

HALO captures the lighter real-world dataset: opponent hero, result, duration, first player and life-event history.

Online practice remains richer

Platforms such as Talishar can preserve deeper turn-by-turn digital data. HALO does not need to imitate that detail at a physical table.

One deck identity, two training environments

Use the same external deck link or deck ID as the common label, then compare paper performance with online practice without pretending they are identical datasets.

DATA BEFORE INTERPRETATION

The app can state what happened reliably. Strategic advice should distinguish measured facts from inferred decisions.

Useful without AI

Numbers first.
AI second.

HALO does not need to build or pay for a proprietary AI model. The app can calculate reliable statistics itself, export a structured analysis pack, and let the player bring it to the AI service they already use.

App calculates facts

Win rate, match duration, life timeline, first/second performance, deck-version record and matchup history.

AI interprets patterns

Players can ask for training priorities, recurring weaknesses or a testing-plan summary grounded in the exported record.

Important boundary

Life data can show damage received. It cannot by itself prove “blocked too little” or “attacked too much.” Those conclusions require richer context and should be labeled as interpretation.

Competitive Team Mode · proposed

This changes who the product can be sold to

One player buys a tracker.
A team buys a training system.

Competitive teams already practice as a group. If every player's HALO is tied to a player profile, linked practice matches can become a shared testing history — with explicit team sharing permissions — so the captain can see what the team has actually tested before a major event.

Community-first distribution

Creator philosophy
