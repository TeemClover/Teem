# CEO Skill Ledger

Last updated: **2026-09-01**, after closing August 2026.

Levels are an internal operating baseline, not a personality score and not a comparison with other CEOs. The purpose is to identify where the next unit of effort creates the most leverage.

Scale:

- **Lv 1–2 — Awareness:** understands the concept, little repeated evidence.
- **Lv 3–4 — Functional:** can perform deliberately but results are inconsistent.
- **Lv 5–6 — Repeatable:** evidence of repeated competence; still needs systems and measurement.
- **Lv 7–8 — Leverage:** strong capability that can create differentiated output and should be systematized/delegated.
- **Lv 9 — Signature:** unusually strong, central founder advantage.
- **Lv 10 — Mastery:** reserved; never assign merely for confidence or effort.

# Highest-priority September level-ups

The company does **not** need to level every skill equally. The strongest skills are already far ahead of the bottleneck. September XP should concentrate here:

## 1. Distribution Strategy — Lv 3 → Lv 4

### Definition of level-up

At least one acquisition path is observed more than once:

`source → content/invite → visit/conversation → first value → next action`

The team can state:

- who the person was;
- where they came from;
- what made them stop/pay attention;
- what first value they received;
- what they did next;
- whether the path can be repeated.

### XP missions

- Choose 1–2 audiences, not the whole ecosystem.
- Track source for every meaningful new conversation.
- Review the path weekly and remove steps rather than adding destinations.

---

## 2. Hook / Short-form Content Psychology — Lv 3 → Lv 4

### Definition of level-up

The team can distinguish a topic problem from a packaging/hook problem using controlled tests and account-specific metrics.

### XP missions

- Pick at most 3 recurring topics.
- Write 10 hooks per topic before premium production.
- Test with phone footage, screen recording, existing footage, simple voice/text, or other cheap formats.
- Record early retention/scroll-stop signal where available, completion/retention, shares/saves/comments, profile visits, follow/lead action.
- Re-edit a failed premium video using only a new hook/opening and compare performance.
- Promote winners into better production only after evidence.

### Boss fight

Create a content format that can be repeated without celebrity piggybacking and that viewers can describe in one sentence: “I follow this account because I consistently get ___.”

## 3. Experiment Design — Lv 4 → Lv 5

### Definition of level-up

Major experiments have a written hypothesis, cheap falsification step, controlled variables, expected signal, and stop rule before large spend.

### Required template

**Cheapest meaningful test:**

**Primary signal:**  
___

**Failure / stop rule:**  
___

**What stays constant:**  
___

**What changes:**  
___

**If it wins:**  
___

**If it loses:**  
___

## 4. Customer Discovery — Lv 4 → Lv 5

### Definition of level-up

Product changes can point to specific observed friction from real people, not only founder intuition.

### XP missions

- Observe at least one real user or buyer journey every week.
- Write the behavior and quote/observation before proposing the feature.
- Separate “user could not understand” from “user understood but did not care.”
- For TeamBook, specifically observe whether people return after first visibility/seen behavior and why.

---

## 5. CEO Capital Allocation — Lv 4 → Lv 5

### Definition of level-up

Substantial spend and founder time are routinely tied to an expected return in learning, revenue, retention, acquisition, or reusable capability.

### XP missions

Before any task likely to exceed half a day or any meaningful paid tool/credit spend, write:

1. What outcome can change?
2. Why is this the cheapest sufficient way to learn/change it?
3. What would make us stop?
4. Is this fixing a bottleneck or merely exercising a strength?

### September warning

The August Higgsfield cost is a **sunk cost**. Do not generate more simply to “earn it back.” Recover the investment by using the learned production capability and existing assets in a more profitable loop.

# Signature strengths to protect, not overfeed

These skills remain strategic advantages but should receive less incremental CEO time in September unless directly attached to a validated bottleneck:

- Systems architecture
- Rapid prototyping
- Product polish
- Technical debugging
- AI visual production
- World/story building

The failure mode of a strong founder is often not weakness. It is **overusing the strongest tool because it feels productive and reliable**.

# September level-up scoreboard

At the end of September, do not award a level merely because more work was done. Require evidence.

Questions for monthly close:

1. Which acquisition path repeated?
2. Which content hypothesis was falsified?
3. Which content format beat the account baseline more than once?
4. What did a real user do that changed a product decision?
5. What was sold and how many times did the sales path repeat?
6. Which recurring CEO task became a test, standard, automation, or delegated process?
7. Which expensive activity was stopped because evidence was weak?
8. What did we deliberately **not build**?

That is XP.
